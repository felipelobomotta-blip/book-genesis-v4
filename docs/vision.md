# Vision

## More authors, more original stories

Writing a book has always required access: time, craft knowledge, feedback, tools, and the confidence to keep revising after a first draft disappoints. Generative models can lower some of those barriers, but a button that produces pages is not the same as a creative process.

Book Genesis exists to make the process more accessible without pretending that originality can be automated. The author supplies the spark, the strange connection, the memories, the sense of what matters, and the willingness to reject a convenient answer. The runner gives that work a durable structure: a brief, an outline, chapter attempts, blind reads, audit reports, and a local history that can be examined or changed.

The point is not to manufacture bestsellers. Nobody can honestly guarantee one. The point is to let more people turn a real idea into a reviewable manuscript, then make better decisions about it with evidence rather than model self-congratulation.

## What we believe

Creativity is the scarce ingredient. Models can suggest language and variation; they cannot supply a person's stakes, taste, cultural knowledge, or responsibility for a claim. Book Genesis treats the human as author and editor, not as a prompt dispenser.

Good feedback needs distance. A writer should not be the only judge of its own work. The runner therefore separates writing, blind chapter reading, revision comparison, and manuscript-level audit whenever the configured providers allow it. When only one model family is available, the run records that weaker independence rather than hiding it.

Provenance matters. A clean-looking final file is not enough if nobody can tell which draft was read, why it changed, or whether an audit actually passed. Book Genesis preserves attempts and acceptance links so a reader can inspect what happened.

Claims need proportional evidence. A model-reader score is a process signal, not proof that people will love a book. A passed audit is useful editorial information, not a publication decision. Human readers, editors, and the market remain outside the software and must be treated as such.

## What we are building

An open local writing environment where one idea can become a structured manuscript through any provider the author can access, including manual copy-and-paste workflows. It should be usable by a novelist with a terminal, a teacher helping a class prototype stories, an indie creator with a local model, and a developer experimenting with narrative systems.

It should reward specificity over generic output. Its useful unit is not “a book generated in one click”; it is a sequence of author decisions that survives interruption, critique, and revision.

## What success looks like

Success is not a dashboard score. It is a new author reaching a coherent first manuscript without losing ownership; a reader identifying a specific moment they remember; a revision changing something real; and a community making the tools, genre profiles, prompts, and safeguards better in public.

The technical beta is not there yet. We still need observed onboarding with new authors, blind human-reader comparisons, and long-form runs that test continuity over many chapters. Those are product claims to earn, not launch copy to invent.

## Invitation

Bring the idea that is too personal, too strange, too niche, or too early for a traditional process. Keep the parts only you can make. Use Book Genesis to put the work on the page, test it, argue with it, and keep going.
